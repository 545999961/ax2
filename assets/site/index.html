01 / Research question

Learning to improve,
round after round.

AREX-2 learns from trajectories of attempts, feedback, and revisions to judge what to change and turn more rounds into better solutions.

Reflection uses feedback to guide better revisions.
Long-horizon execution retains useful findings and recovers from setbacks across rounds.
Long-horizon reflection combines these capabilities to sustain improvement within a task.

02 / The ideaOne task · Many rounds

Agents that improve
within the task.

Reflection guides the next revision.
Long-horizon execution keeps the loop productive.

03 / Training data

For coding tasks.
Cross-domain transfer.

Machine learning engineering and algorithmic programming offer verifiable feedback and room for sustained improvement. We use both to teach long-horizon reflection.

Constructing Environments

A teacher model turns GitHub repositories and online-judge problems into executable environments with scoring functions.

Reflective trajectories

A teacher agent uses feedback and operational knowledge to improve solutions over many rounds.

Select and train

Select whole trajectories by their final outcome and process quality. Train on the decisions that move the solution forward.

04 / Results

Strong in coding.
Transfer to deep research.

AREX-2 (27B) improves over both previous AREX models on all four deep-research benchmarks, using the same deep-research training data. The results support transfer of long-horizon reflection beyond its source domains.
